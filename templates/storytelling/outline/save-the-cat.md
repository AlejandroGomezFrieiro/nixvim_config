# Save the Cat (Blake Snyder)

A beat sheet for structurally sound screenplays/stories. Grease the
numbers — beats flex with runtime.

## The Beats

1. [ ] **Opening Image (1)** — a shot of who the hero is *now*
2. [ ] **Theme Stated (5)** — the lesson, said aloud by someone else
3. [ ] **Set-up (1–10)** — who's who, the ordinary world, the flaw on display
4. [ ] **Catalyst (12)** — the shape of things changes; nothing stays the same
5. [ ] **Debate (12–25)** — "should I? can I?" voiced in a few scenes
6. [ ] **Break into Two (25)** — the hero *chooses* the new adventure, bag packed
7. [ ] **B Story (30)** — a subplot (often love/mentor) that mirrors the theme
8. [ ] **Fun and Games (30–55)** — the trailer: the premise delivering
9. [ ] **Midpoint (55)** — stakes double; a win (or a loss) that flips the game
10. [ ] **Bad Guys Close In (55–75)** — pressure mounts, the plan frays, B Story weaves
11. [ ] **All Is Lost (75)** — the "whiff of death"; the old plan is dead
12. [ ] **Dark Night of the Soul (75–85)** — grieve the failure; find the lesson
13. [ ] **Break into Three (85)** — the new resolve, the B Story merges
14. [ ] **Finale (85–110)** — the plan works because the lesson is applied
15. [ ] **Final Image (110)** — the hero *now*, echoing the opening image
