# Three-Act Structure

The classical model. Each beat is one story event, not one scene.

## Act I — Setup (approx. 25%)

- [ ] **Opening image** — protagonist's ordinary world, one frame that says it all
- [ ] **Thesis stated** — the lie or strategy they live by appears in dialogue
- [ ] **Inciting incident** — the change cracks the ordinary world open
- [ ] **Debate** — should they step forward? Resolve it loudly
- [ ] **Call to adventure accepted** — first deliberate choice toward the new world

## Act II — Confrontation (approx. 50%)

- [ ] **Promise of the premise** — the fun of the world doing its thing
- [ ] **Rising action** — failures teach them, each attempt costs more
- [ ] **Midpoint reversal** — dent in the armour; stakes visibly double
- [ ] **Dark moment before the midpoint echoes** — the plan is exposed
- [ ] **All is lost** — a death, betrayal, or the strategy proving hollow

## Act III — Resolution (approx. 25%)

- [ ] **Darkest hour** — the antagonist seems to have won
- [ ] **Thesis/antithesis clash** — external fight *and* internal give-in
- [ ] **Synthesis** — the new person applies what they learned
- [ ] **Resolution echo** — the opening image, now changed
- [ ] **False to true normal** — the world breathes again
